# Card Game Design

Design educational card games that a teacher can print, cut, and run in a 40-minute class. The output is a playable game: deck list, setup, rules, special cards, win condition, and a note on how to make it easier or harder.

## Design stance

- Start from the learning objective and the classroom gap it addresses, then pick the win condition. The card interaction itself must exercise the target knowledge or skill.
- Classrooms have 20-30 students. Default to teams of 4-5 playing as one hand, or a tournament bracket, rather than individual play.
- Blend chance and skill. Pure chance (bingo style) suits young children and memorization; strategy suits older students and deeper thinking.
- Design for interaction: trading, challenges, alliances. Physical cards are social by nature, use that.
- Keep the base game simple and add optional advanced rules for older or experienced players.

## Mechanics that work in class

- **Challenge duel**: a player challenges another with a question from the card content. Correct answer keeps the cards, wrong answer forfeits one. Learning is the mechanic, and it adds risk.
- **Blind trade**: exchange face-down cards. Creates hidden information and alliances.
- **Help system**: a player who helps a struggling peer gets a small bonus. Keeps weaker players in the game.
- **Special cards**: 2-6 per deck, named inside the theme, rare enough to feel special, never strong enough to win alone. Archetypes: Explorer (draw from everyone), Spy (reveal a hand), Conqueror (take a card type), Architect (force a set), Shield (block one challenge), Sage (name a card, holder surrenders it).

## Card design rules

- One visual concept for the whole deck. Card type must be recognizable at a glance, with distinct but harmonious colors for categories.
- Minimal text. Icons, colors and symbols over sentences. A card should be scannable in seconds.
- Each card carries an information layer (the content) and a mechanic layer (what it does).

## Flash cards and classroom card systems

- Flash card activities: recognition match, speed challenge, set collection by trading, challenge duels with a third student as judge, progression where harder cards unlock after mastery.
- Feedback cards: replace yellow/red penalty cards with reward cards (Super Card, Hero Card). Teacher hands them out for desired behavior, students can give them to each other at day's end, and the top collector gets recognition. This inverts the disciplinary model.
- Green card system: reward helpfulness and fair play, track over a term, recognize recipients.

## Playtest before shipping

Play it yourself, then with 4-6 students of the target age, observe without intervening, then adjust. Red flags: finishes too fast, one strategy dominates, rules confuse, early elimination, content feels bolted on. If a player can be eliminated early, give them a role (judge, scorekeeper, advisor).

## Anti-patterns

- Fun game plus quiz questions at the end. The content must be the mechanic.
- A single special card that wins the game.
- Reading-heavy cards.
- Every game plays out the same way.

## Done when

A teacher could print the deck tomorrow, explain the rules in two minutes, and the students would be practicing the target content on every turn.
